Three layers

How it protects you

Each layer catches what the one before it can't — block first, blur what slips through, and report the rest to the person you've asked to keep you honest.

Blocks adult sites

A system-wide filter stops known adult sites from loading in any browser or app.

Blurs explicit images

A browser add-on blurs explicit images and video right on the page, as they load.

Watches the whole screen

For everything else, it checks the screen on your machine and reports flagged moments to your partner — with an alert if you try to switch it off.

Setup · about 10 minutes, once

Getting it running on a Mac

  1. Open the app

    Unzip the download and open the app. The first time, it sets itself up and arranges to start quietly every time you log in — no Terminal needed.

  2. Fill in your settings page

    It opens a simple settings page in your browser. Enter your accountability partner's email, your own email, and how often you'd like summaries. A Send test email button lets you confirm it works before you rely on it.

  3. Create a Gmail App Password

    So it can send email from your account, make a one-time App Password and paste it into the settings page.

    myaccount.google.com/apppasswords

  4. Allow it to see the screen

    In System Settings → Privacy & Security → Screen Recording, turn it on for the app. Without this, the screenshots come out blank.

The one step people miss

Step 4, the Screen Recording permission. If your partner's reports come back empty or all-black, that permission isn't on yet — turn it on and reopen the app.

Windows & Linux work the same way

Open the app for your system; the settings page and everything after it are identical. The only Mac-specific part is the Screen Recording permission above.

The promise

Private because there's nowhere else for it to go

Your screen is checked on your own computer and is never uploaded anywhere. When something is flagged, a blurred image is emailed straight from your machine to your partner. There's no company server in the middle holding your screen activity, because there's no company server at all. You run the software; it answers to you.

Questions

Good to know

Is it really free?
Yes. It's open-source under the MIT license, and the full code is public on GitHub. There's nothing to buy and no account to make.
Can I just remove it myself?
You're the admin of your own computer, so yes — but disabling or removing it sends an alert to your partner. It's built to make slipping unnoticed hard, not impossible.
Does it catch everything?
No tool does. It blocks a lot, blurs more, and reports the rest. The detection runs on-device and is best-effort — the honest trade for keeping everything private.
Who can see my screen?
Only the partner you choose, and only blurred images of flagged moments, sent directly to them. Not us — we run nothing and receive nothing.
